The model

The Nimble Gravity AI-SDLC Maturity Model

Six levels, five dimensions, one concrete gate per transition.

The Nimble Gravity AI-SDLC Maturity Model describes how organizations progress from no AI use to an SDLC designed around agents. Internally we call it ORBIT: grounded, then orbit, then escape velocity. A level is not a vibe: it is a set of auditable requirements across five dimensions, and a single concrete event that marks the transition. Either the gate happened or it did not.

The gates

Each transition is marked by one event. The gate is deliberately concrete: anyone can claim a maturity level, but a gate either happened or it did not.

TransitionGateThe insight that moves you
0 → 1
No AI Use to Ad Hoc		First sanctioned license issued and an acceptable-use policy published.Prohibition ends. Risk is managed, not avoided.
1 → 2
Ad Hoc to Defined		First named workflow with its configuration committed to version control, reproduced end to end by a second person.AI use becomes an artifact, not a habit.
2 → 3
Defined to Managed Autonomy		First ticket completed AI-driven end to end, with human approval only at defined checkpoints.Verification shifts from reading code to checking evidence.
3 → 4
Managed Autonomy to Quantitatively Managed		Telemetry is on by default with baselines, and at least one workflow has been retired because the numbers said so.You can only scale what you can kill.
4 → 5
Quantitatively Managed to Optimizing		The SDLC is redesigned assuming agents, and AI-native economics appear in pricing or SoWs.The process is built for agents; humans work by exception.

Measurement starts at Level 1

The model places measurement-driven management at Level 4, but recording starts with the first license. GitHub’s playbooks make baselining the first step of AI adoption, and pre-AI baselines are unrecoverable once adoption spreads. The metrics appendix catalogs what to capture, including how to get ROI signal out of Claude Code, Claude Cowork, and Claude chat.

The five dimensions

Every level states its requirements across the same five dimensions, so any two levels are directly comparable: People & Skills · Process & Workflow · Platform & Tooling · Governance & Risk · Measurement. Roughly three requirements per dimension, each phrased as an auditable statement.
